How to calculate cumulative GPA (CGPA)

Cumulative GPA combines academic results across more than one semester or study period. When semester credit totals differ, weight each semester GPA by the credits included in that semester. The result is only comparable with an official CGPA if the institution uses the same grade scale, credit totals, and course-inclusion rules.

Credit-weighted CGPA formula

CGPA = sum(semester GPA × semester credits) / sum(semester credits)

Each semester's GPA is multiplied by its relevant credits. Add the weighted values and divide by total credits. Use the credits counted toward cumulative GPA, not automatically the number of courses or class meetings.

Worked examples

Example 1: semesters with different credit totals

  1. Semester 1: 15 credits at 3.4 gives 15 × 3.4 = 51 weighted points.
  2. Semester 2: 12 credits at 3.8 gives 12 × 3.8 = 45.6 weighted points.
  3. (51 + 45.6) / (15 + 12) = 96.6 / 27 = 3.577..., or 3.58 to two decimals.

Example 2: equal-credit semesters

  1. Two semesters each carry 15 credits, with GPAs of 3.2 and 3.6.
  2. Calculate (15 × 3.2 + 15 × 3.6) / 30.
  3. (48 + 54) / 30 = 3.40 CGPA. With equal credits, this also equals (3.2 + 3.6) / 2.

Steps for checking a cumulative result

  1. Collect the GPA and GPA-counted credit total for each completed semester from your academic record.
  2. Confirm that all semester GPAs use the same compatible scale and precision.
  3. Multiply each GPA by its credits, add those products, and divide by all included credits.
  4. Compare using the rounding and repeat-course rules your institution publishes.

Students in Nepal should use their university or college's current academic regulations and transcript information. Retakes, withdrawals, transferred courses, or repeated credits can be handled differently by different institutions.

Common mistakes

  • Averaging semester GPAs equally when credit totals differ.
  • Entering a semester's course count instead of its GPA-counted credit total.
  • Combining GPAs from incompatible scales or rounded values without checking the source records.
  • Assuming a retake or transfer is counted the same way at every institution.

Frequently asked questions

Is CGPA just the average of semester GPAs?

It is a weighted average when semesters have different credits. A simple average works only when each semester carries equal weight under the institution's rules.

Can I use a percentage instead of semester GPA?

Only if your institution provides an approved conversion. Do not invent a universal percentage-to-GPA conversion.

How is this different from semester GPA?

Semester GPA covers one period of study; CGPA combines the included periods. See the GPA calculation guide.
