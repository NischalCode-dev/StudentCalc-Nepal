How to calculate the marks you need in a final exam

If a course combines internal assessment and a final examination by percentage weights, you can work backward from an overall target to estimate the final score required. This is a planning calculation, not a prediction or a guarantee: use the assessment structure and rounding rules provided by your teacher or institution.

Weighted final-score formula

Let the internal score and final-exam score be percentages from 0 to 100. If the final exam has weight w percent, the internal component has weight 100 - w percent:

Overall = internal score × (1 - w / 100) + final score × (w / 100)

Rearranging gives Required final score = (target overall - internal score × (1 - w / 100)) / (w / 100). The calculator assumes the internal and final components together make up the whole grade.

Worked examples

Example 1: final worth 60%

  1. Internal performance is 80%, the final exam weight is 60%, and the target overall is 70%.
  2. The internal share is 40%, so its contribution is 80 × 0.40 = 32 percentage points.
  3. Required final = (70 - 32) / 0.60 = 38 / 0.60 = 63.33%.

Example 2: final worth half the grade

  1. Internal performance is 68%; the final exam is worth 50%; the target overall is 75%.
  2. The internal contribution is 68 × 0.50 = 34 points.
  3. Required final = (75 - 34) / 0.50 = 82%.

Using the estimate to plan

Students preparing for school, NEB, or university assessments can use the calculation to turn a target into a concrete practice goal. First confirm whether the internal input should be a percentage rather than raw marks, and whether the stated final weight applies to the whole course. If the answer exceeds 100%, the target is mathematically unreachable under these inputs; discuss realistic next steps with your teacher rather than changing the official weights.

Common mistakes

  • Entering raw internal marks when the formula requires the internal component's percentage.
  • Entering the internal weight where the calculator asks for the final-exam weight.
  • Forgetting that the internal share is the remainder: 100% minus final weight.
  • Using this two-part formula for courses with extra components, caps, bonus marks, or different rules.

Frequently asked questions

What if my required final score is over 100%?

With the entered scores and weights, the target cannot be reached by the final exam alone. Verify the inputs and ask your institution about applicable course rules.

What if my internal component is out of 25 marks?

Convert it to a percentage first if the calculator expects a percentage. For example, 20 out of 25 is 80%. Use the institution's weighting to check that this conversion matches the course model.

Does the result state a pass mark?

No. It estimates the final score for the overall target you enter; it does not determine official pass requirements.
