GPA & grades

How to calculate GPA with course credits

A grade point average (GPA) summarizes the grade points earned across courses. When courses carry different credit hours, a credit-weighted average gives each course influence in proportion to its credits. This is useful for checking a semester result, but the grade-point scale and the credits counted must come from your own institution.

The weighted GPA formula

GPA = sum(grade point × course credits) / sum(course credits)

First multiply every course's grade point by its credit value. Add those weighted points, then divide by the total credits included. GPA is not the same as the arithmetic mean of letter grades or raw marks.

Worked examples

Example 1: two courses with different credits

  1. A 3-credit course has a grade point of 3.6: 3 × 3.6 = 10.8 weighted points.
  2. A 1-credit course has a grade point of 4.0: 1 × 4.0 = 4.0 weighted points.
  3. Divide their total by total credits: (10.8 + 4.0) / (3 + 1) = 14.8 / 4 = 3.70 GPA.

Example 2: a second set of course results

  1. A 3-credit course at 3.2 contributes 9.6 weighted points; a 2-credit course at 3.8 contributes 7.6.
  2. Add the contributions and credits: 9.6 + 7.6 = 17.2 points and 3 + 2 = 5 credits.
  3. 17.2 / 5 = 3.44 GPA.

How to calculate your result

  1. Use your institution's transcript or handbook to find each course's grade point and credits.
  2. Check which courses and credits count toward the semester GPA; do not assume every institution uses the same rules.
  3. Multiply each grade point by credits, add the products, and divide by the included credits.
  4. Apply your institution's stated rounding rule when comparing your manual result with a transcript.

For students in Nepal, a university's published grading scheme is the source to use. The GPA calculator's standard scale is a generic option; its custom mode lets you enter points from your institution, but it does not verify the scale or transcript rules.

Common mistakes

  • Taking a simple mean when course credits differ.
  • Mixing raw marks, letter grades, and grade points as if they were interchangeable.
  • Leaving out a course or using credits that your institution does not count toward GPA.
  • Rounding individual values early instead of applying the institution's final rounding policy.

Frequently asked questions

Is GPA always on a 4.0 scale?

No. Institutions set their own grading scales. Check your current university guidance and enter the corresponding grade points.

When is a simple average enough?

It matches a credit-weighted average when all included courses have equal credits. If credits differ, use the weighted formula.

Does this calculate CGPA?

This page covers a set of courses, such as a semester. To combine completed semesters by their credits, use the CGPA guide.
