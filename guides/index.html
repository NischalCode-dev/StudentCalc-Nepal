Understand the numbers behind your study plan.

These short guides explain common academic calculations with worked examples. Institutional grading, attendance, and assessment rules can differ, so use the latest guidance from your school or examination authority whenever those rules matter.

GPA, SGPA, and CGPA: what is weighted?

GPA summarizes grade points for a set of courses. SGPA usually refers to one semester, while CGPA combines results across semesters. When courses carry different credit hours, multiply each grade point by its credits, add those products, then divide by the total credits.

Example: A 3-credit course at 3.6 and a 1-credit course at 4.0 give (3 × 3.6 + 1 × 4.0) / 4 = 3.7. The arithmetic does not define the grade-point scale; your institution does.

How to calculate a percentage from marks

Divide the marks earned by the maximum possible marks, then multiply by 100. Keep obtained and maximum marks from the same assessment or combine both totals before calculating.

Example: 45 out of 60 is 45 / 60 × 100 = 75%. A percentage compares a score to its maximum but does not account for assessment difficulty or a grading policy.

Understand attendance requirements

Attendance percentage is classes attended divided by classes counted as conducted, multiplied by 100. Use the same class-counting rule as your school, especially for cancelled sessions, labs, or multiple periods.

Example: 18 attended out of 24 is 75%. To reach 80% by attending every future class, solve (18 + x) / (24 + x) = 0.80; x = 6. This estimate assumes no further absences.

Estimate the marks needed in a final exam

When an internal component and final exam have different weights, calculate their weighted contributions. If the internal score is 80% and counts for 40% of the course, it contributes 80 × 0.40 = 32 percentage points.

Example: For a 70% overall target with the final worth 60%, the required final is (70 − 32) / 0.60 = 63.34%. This assumes the two weights sum to 100%; use your course's actual assessment structure.

How to read an NEB GPA estimate

A GPA estimate depends on the subject grade points, theory/practical assessment split, and credit hours used in the calculation. StudentCalc's NEB tool displays subject-level points, credits, and weighted contributions so you can inspect its assumptions.

Its current implementation uses an encoded grade-point table, 3- or 5-credit subject values, and 75/25 assessment weighting for most subjects, with a 50/50 split for Computer Science and Hotel Management. Check the current rules and credits for your programme; this estimate is not an official NEB result.

Understanding the SEE GPA calculator's assumptions

The SEE tool asks for theory and practical grades and applies the grade points shown on the page. Its current calculation uses a 75/25 split for each subject and takes an equal average of the eight subject points; it does not apply subject credit hours.

This describes the website's calculation model, not an official grading rule. Confirm the current subject combination, grade-point mapping, weighting, and any credit-hour treatment with your school or the examination authority before relying on an estimate.

Make a realistic study plan

Start by listing exam dates, assignment deadlines, and topics you need to cover. Break large topics into short tasks, schedule focused practice and review, and leave time to rest. A balanced split can be a starting point, but challenging or urgent subjects may need more time.

After a study block, note what you completed and adjust the next block rather than assuming every task takes the same time. The calculator divides minutes evenly and does not include breaks.
