NEB grading: what the website calculator assumes

“NEB grading system” can refer to official, programme-specific rules that are set by the relevant authorities and may change. This page does not claim to reproduce those rules. It documents the assumptions currently encoded in StudentCalc Nepal's NEB GPA calculator so students can interpret its estimate and check it against current official materials.

Grade points and subject weights in the calculator

The website model maps the displayed grades as follows: A+ = 4.0, A = 3.6, B+ = 3.2, B = 2.8, C+ = 2.4, C = 2.0, D = 1.6, and NG = 0. These are the calculator's encoded values, not a representation of an official NEB grade table.

For most subjects, the calculator uses a 75/25 theory-to-practical or internal split. Computer Science and Hotel Management use a 50/50 split in this implementation. It assigns 3 credits to English, Nepali, and Social Studies and Life Skills Education, and 5 credits to other listed subjects. The selectable subjects are a simplified Science or Management stream setup.

Its calculations are subject point = (theory point × theory weight + practical/internal point × practical/internal weight) / 100 and GPA = sum(subject point × encoded subject credits) / total encoded credits. Check every scale, component share, subject list, and credit against current official guidance before using a result for an academic decision.

Worked examples using this website model

Example 1: a subject with a 75/25 split

  1. Assume theory grade B is 2.8 and practical/internal grade A is 3.6 on this calculator's table.
  2. Use the 75/25 split: (2.8 × 75 + 3.6 × 25) / 100.
  3. (210 + 90) / 100 = 3.0 subject points.

Example 2: combine two differently credited subjects

  1. Take the 3-credit subject at 3.0 points from Example 1: 3 × 3.0 = 9 weighted points.
  2. A 5-credit subject at 3.6 contributes 5 × 3.6 = 18 weighted points.
  3. (9 + 18) / (3 + 5) = 27 / 8 = 3.375, displayed by the calculator to two decimals as 3.38 GPA.

How students can use the estimate

For practice or an arithmetic check, choose the calculator's stream and subjects, enter the component grades shown on the relevant record, then inspect the displayed subject points and weighted contributions. Do not assume the calculator's subject selection or encoded credits match your programme. In Nepal, use current notices and official documents from your school or the relevant examination authority to determine the grading policy that applies to you.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming the website's mappings, subject options, or credits are official NEB rules.
  • Applying the same component split to every subject; this implementation encodes two exceptions.
  • Using a subject both as the selected choice and as an optional subject.
  • Comparing the unrounded intermediate result to a transcript without checking the official rounding method.

Frequently asked questions

Does this page state the current official NEB grading system?

No. It describes only the calculation logic in this website. Confirm current official grading, subject, component, and credit rules with the relevant school or examination authority.

Why can the result differ from a marksheet?

The official subject combination, grade conversion, credit assignment, component treatment, or rounding may differ from the calculator's assumptions.

Which subjects use a 50/50 component split in this tool?

Computer Science and Hotel Management use 50/50 in the current implementation; most other listed subjects use 75/25.
