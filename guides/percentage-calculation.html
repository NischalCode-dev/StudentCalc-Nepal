How to calculate a percentage from marks

A percentage expresses a score as a fraction of the maximum possible score, scaled to 100. It helps students compare performance across papers with different maximum marks, but it does not by itself determine a grade, pass status, or final course result.

The percentage formula

Percentage = (obtained marks / maximum marks) × 100

Use marks from the same assessment or combine compatible components by adding both the marks earned and their maximums. The maximum must be greater than zero, and the obtained marks cannot exceed it for this calculator.

Worked examples

Example 1: one test

  1. You earned 45 marks out of 60.
  2. Divide: 45 / 60 = 0.75.
  3. Multiply by 100: 0.75 × 100 = 75%.

Example 2: combine two papers with different maximums

  1. You earned 18 out of 25 on one paper and 42 out of 50 on another.
  2. Add earned marks: 18 + 42 = 60. Add maximum marks: 25 + 50 = 75.
  3. Calculate 60 / 75 × 100 = 80%.

This combined-score method weights each mark point equally. If a course assigns explicit component weightages, calculate the weighted result using those rules instead of treating raw marks as one combined total.

Applying percentages to study in Nepal

Students can use the formula to check a class test, practical assessment, or combined internal marks. Keep the paper maximum and assessment structure from the teacher or school record. A percentage does not automatically translate into a letter grade or GPA: consult your institution's current scale for that conversion.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing maximum marks by obtained marks instead of the other way around.
  • Adding marks earned but forgetting to add the maximum marks for all included papers.
  • Combining raw marks when the course components have separate weightages.
  • Assuming the same percentage means the same official grade across every school or examination.

Frequently asked questions

Can a percentage have decimal places?

Yes. The calculation may produce a decimal; use the rounding convention stated by your course or institution when reporting it.

Can I convert a percentage directly to GPA?

Not with a universal formula. Grade-point conversion depends on the scale your institution uses. See the GPA guide and check the applicable scale.

How do I work out a weighted exam total?

Apply each component's percentage weight to its score, then add the weighted contributions. The exam marks guide shows how to solve for a needed final score.
