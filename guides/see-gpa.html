Understanding the SEE GPA calculator

This guide explains the calculation currently implemented in the StudentCalc Nepal SEE tool. It is a website estimate, not a statement of official SEE policy or a substitute for an issued marksheet. Subject combinations, grading rules, component weighting, and result treatment must be checked against current guidance from your school or the examination authority.

What the website calculator does

The tool applies the following displayed grade-point values to theory and practical grades:

For each of the six compulsory and two optional subjects, the website model calculates subject point = (theory point × 75 + practical point × 25) / 100. It then calculates GPA = sum of the eight subject points / 8. Each subject is equally weighted in this model; it does not apply subject credit hours. If either component is NG, the tool marks that subject point unavailable and does not produce an overall GPA.

Worked examples using this website model

Example 1: combine theory and practical points

  1. On the displayed calculator scale, A is 3.6 and B is 2.8.
  2. For theory A and practical B, calculate (3.6 × 75 + 2.8 × 25) / 100.
  3. (270 + 70) / 100 = 3.4 subject points.

Example 2: average eight subject points

  1. Suppose seven entered subjects each have a calculated point of 3.6 and one has 3.2.
  2. Add them: (7 × 3.6) + 3.2 = 28.4.
  3. Divide by eight: 28.4 / 8 = 3.55 GPA before any institution-specific treatment.

Using the estimate for SEE preparation

You can use the tool to understand how different entered component grades affect a modeled average or to check your arithmetic against a set of practice results. Compare the subjects and component results with records supplied by your school. For an official result, rely on the current published SEE rules and your official marksheet, not a website estimate.

Common mistakes

  • Reversing theory and practical entries even though the website assigns them different weights.
  • Reading the calculator's table as an official or guaranteed current grade-point scale.
  • Assuming all schools or official result calculations treat subjects equally or exclude credits.
  • Treating NG as zero; the SEE calculator has no numeric point for NG and reports the GPA unavailable.

Frequently asked questions

Is the result an official SEE GPA?

No. It is produced from the calculator's own displayed table, weighting, subject inputs, and equal-average rule. Confirm official grading and result calculation with your school or the examination authority.

Does the calculator use subject credits?

No. Its current model equally averages the eight calculated subject points. Official subject or credit treatment may differ.

Why does NG make the overall result unavailable?

The calculator implementation assigns NG no numeric point, so it cannot include that component in the numeric estimate.
